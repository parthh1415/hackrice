Reading it

Green is stable — a shock dies out. Red is where forced selling feeds itself. The edge between them is the critical leverage, and it falls as crowding rises: the more alike these books are, the less leverage it takes to tip over.

Nothing about the assets changes across this map. Only the structure does.

Caccioli, Shrestha, Moore & Farmer (J. Banking & Finance, 2014) derive this boundary analytically. This one is computed from filed positions.

Assumptions & what this does not claim
  • Holdings are real. loading…. Anyone can reproduce them from EDGAR.
  • Leverage is not. No fund discloses it. It's the slider above, applied uniformly, and every number on this page moves when you change it.
  • Price impact is a model, not a measurement. Δp/p = −γ · (dollars sold) / ADV, linear in participation. γ is the second slider. At γ=0 there is no contagion and amplification is exactly 1.00 — that's the control.
  • ADV figures are order-of-magnitude: …
  • 13F is long-only US equity, quarterly, filed 45 days late. Options and bond principal rows are filtered out. Shorts and derivatives are invisible to us.
  • We do not predict market moves. This computes a stability property of a declared configuration. The shock it finds is the smallest one our search found under these assumptions.
